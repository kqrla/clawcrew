# 35 · talking across a boundary without losing the argument

A2A (agent cards, tasks, artifacts, streaming) solves transport between agents that do not share a
runtime. it does not define what a *claim* is, so it cannot prevent the failure that matters to a
decoupled org: two seats merge their work into a summary that drops the disagreement, the uncertainty,
and the reason.

## what a message carries

`src/domain/discourse.ts`. a message has an intent, claims, open questions, and references. a claim
has a kind (`fact` / `inference` / `preference` / `plan`), a statement, an optional evidence
reference, a confidence, a basis, an author, and an explicit list of the claims it argues against.

the refusals are where the value is:

| rule | refuses |
| --- | --- |
| `fact_without_evidence` | a fact with no resolvable reference |
| `plan_stated_as_fact` | a plan at 0.9 confidence, which is the overclaim the w-framework names |
| `confidence_out_of_range` | a number pretending to be a probability |
| `opposes_an_absent_claim` | arguing with something not in the room |
| `message_says_nothing` | a handoff with no claim and no question |

## the conservation rule

a synthesis returns four buckets: `kept`, `challenged`, `dropped`, `unresolved`. every input claim
appears in exactly one of them, and `claim_not_accounted_for` fires if it does not. this is the whole
mechanism. nuance does not survive because a prompt asked for it; it survives because losing a claim
is a detectable fault.

three consequences worth naming:

- **opposition is kept, not resolved.** a claim somebody argues against lands in `challenged` and
  stays there with its opposing claim. a merge is not a place to settle an argument.
- **you cannot discard a sourced claim on an unsourced objection.** dropping one requires a rebuttal
  that is itself a reference (`rebuttal_needs_evidence`), or the claim stays.
- **agreement is not evidence.** two seats asserting the same unsourced thing appear in
  `agreementWithoutEvidence`, which is reported precisely because it feels like corroboration and
  is not.

## what is not built

the wire itself. there is no A2A transport here yet, no agent card endpoint, no streaming. what
exists is the part a transport cannot supply: the claim, and the refusal to lose one. wiring A2A to
this envelope is mechanical; designing a merge that keeps dissent is not, which is why it came first.

## what the A2A spec settles, per the mastra explainer

three things worth taking, none of them code:

**a message can be missed, an artifact cannot.** messages are turns and a reconnect may skip the ones
sent while the socket was down; artifacts live in the task. so a claim that matters is written against
the task, not carried only on the wire. our envelope already separates the two, and this is the reason
to keep them separate: nuance lost to a dropped connection is the same failure as nuance lost to a
flattening merge.

**the card is the permission list.** an agent card at `/.well-known/agent-card.json` declares skills,
formats and credentials before work is sent. for us a seat's card is its slice: the surfaces it
provides and the tools those surfaces imply, which is exactly what `toolsForSlice` already computes.
a card that advertised a surface its slice does not provide would be the `engine_tool_claims_a_surface`
lie with a URL.

**the task is the unit, and it has states.** eight of them in the spec, including the one where work
waits for a human. our work rows already carry states and a lease; mapping ours onto theirs is a
documentation exercise, not a migration.

what it does not settle is anything about meaning: two agents exchanging perfectly formed A2A tasks can
still converge on a summary that has lost the argument. that is `discourse.ts`, and it is why the
envelope exists on top of the transport rather than instead of it.
